# Four-hour firmware progress and efficiency review — October 8, 2026

Review window: **4:46:43–8:46:43 p.m. America/Chicago, October 8**. This is the four hours immediately preceding the review's first clock reading. Work in other chats continued during inspection; results completed after this cutoff are excluded from the window totals. This review launches no firmware and changes no runtime, original input, or completed experiment evidence.

**Verdict:** the project made substantial engineering progress. Its efficiency remains poor in several preventable ways: obsolete observation limits, fragile supervision, copied harness configuration, fragmented experiment planning, and failure to promote important retained evidence into the active status. The harshest criticism is that its documentation machinery can preserve thousands of events while forgetting what the project has already accomplished. Usable original UI remains unachieved.

The claim that 95% of this window was administration is unsupported. Retained supervision records identify **21 completed guests and one in progress at the cutoff**, with **78.891 minutes** from guest creation to recorded reaping or cutoff. The nonoverlapping union is also 78.891 minutes, approximately 32.9% of the four-hour window. The remaining time cannot be allocated between engineering, controls, analysis, reporting, waiting, and pauses from these records. Guest lifetime includes stalls and is an upper bound on actual execution time. **Five of the 21 completed guests lack native finals.** Completed supervisors recorded **36,999 process-table queries**. These counts measure activity and reliability, not useful research output or guest CPU time lost.

**The most damaging failures.**

1. **The project lost its own milestone evidence.** The two whole-main cooperative runs already have byte-validated constructor entry, saved caller, pop-frame, restored stack and caller-return observations. Their original executable hash and six constructor/caller instruction words match the retained original. Their later failures do not retroactively invalidate those earlier observations. Nevertheless, the README inspected at this checkpoint omits both runs. The subsequent CSV/quantum reviews correctly report missing constructor progress in their own runs, but omit the earlier cooperative results when discussing the construction route and future cooperative validation. The candidate's static summary still says firmware validation is unestablished, while its partial outcomes explicitly warn that this text predates actual firmware execution. This distinction was missed downstream.

   Re-observing that constructor under a different scheduling condition is legitimate. Treating its completion as globally unobserved is incorrect. The missing lesson was: **whole-main cooperative scheduling reached it quickly, then encountered separate reliability failures**. The latest retained counted condition subsequently reached it more slowly. No controlled cross-condition speed ratio can be claimed from those different runs. Two failed runs with important positive evidence have no concise HANDOFF, so a workflow that starts only from README and latest HANDOFF systematically misses them. One short failed-run note would have been more valuable than another broad review of better-indexed evidence.

2. **The experiment's real duration was not tested before spending its guest budget.** The launcher armed 605/608/610 deadlines, but the actual watchdog's spawn path tightened them to 300/303/305. Outgoing-argument controls passed while the effective contract was wrong. This is a test-design failure, not evidence that more general checks were needed. Five minutes of additional authorized observation were lost, and a consumed attempt lacked its native final. Earlier 180-second plans also kept stopping before the counted condition's later 197/234-second boundaries. Immediate comparison prompts explicitly authorized 180 seconds, so those launchers followed their immediate scope; the recurring planning default was the defect. [Duration causal review] `2026-10-08-runtime-limit-persistence.md` (evidence not published), [actual shortened run] `../experiments/milestone-following-20261008/run/attempt-supervision.json` (evidence not published).

   The correction now uses a shared explicit duration and actual-spawn supervision controls. That is the right direction. Its longer-runtime firmware behavior must be judged from subsequent completed evidence, not this window's truncated arm. Keep the user's selected duration through new chats and generated plans; reject a total-budget conflict explicitly rather than silently shortening the run.

3. **Harness reliability is consuming experimental opportunities.** Five missing finals are spread across the worker-delivered timeout, cooperative watchdog failure, later cooperative emulator fault/stop, CSV gate failure/stop, and shortened longer run. They do not all have one cause. Focused controls repaired several problems, and owned closure/postflight was retained. Yet the record shows repeated failures at integration boundaries: real syscall mapper names, real guest spawn, native worker error propagation, and effective shutdown. A passing small mock is insufficient when it models the wrong interface. Test the changed integration boundary once, using the actual installed runtime or a harmless owned child where appropriate; reuse the result while its code remains unchanged.

4. **Planning and reporting are still too fragmented.** The valid two-arm comparison took about **10.74 minutes** of its chat turn for approximately **6.02 minutes** of guest lifetime; roughly 4.72 minutes remained for preparation, controls, analysis and reporting. That remainder includes necessary engineering. The subsequent requested review took **7.75 minutes**, longer than both guests together. A prompt, duration discussion, and revised prompt added another **4.15 minutes of assistant turn time**, before the next execution chat. These were explicit user requests and should not be characterized as disobedient administration. They demonstrate the cost of the review/prompt/new-chat relay as the normal operating architecture. The user should not need to be a manual message broker for every adjacent experiment.

5. **There is too much mutable harness assembly.** The fresh-arm generator edits a historical launcher using a series of exact string replacements and copies many modules into each arm. Immutable historical experiments and fresh writable guest state are sensible. Ad hoc transformation of old launchers lets hidden defaults and stale helper copies travel forward. Stabilize one working harness revision with explicit parameters and a small run manifest; snapshot that revision for evidence. The new common harness and shared duration object already reduce this problem. Complete the current research before undertaking a broad framework rewrite.

6. **Measurement volume can masquerade as explanation.** All 36,999 process-table queries were real host work. The supervisor-only candidate reduced queries by 68% and state writes by 93%, but its firmware benefit remains unmeasured and its longer routine census gap changes detection behavior. The retained firmware supervisor still samples processes around every 0.1 seconds and persists state in its 25ms loop. Likewise, the main sample constructs and serializes several observer summaries every five seconds, and `event()` JSON-round-trips the event before printing it. Those are concrete potential costs, not measured dominant bottlenecks. The pixel-leaf experiment's 64,917 returns and 7,095 boosted slices did not help: an observed layout was reached approximately 40 seconds later. Counters cannot compensate for a slower outcome. **What the project is doing well.**

- It now runs substantial firmware experiments and has demonstrated both earlier startup and additional original construction boundaries. The four-hour window was productive.
- It compares fresh copies of a fixed seed, changes one scheduling condition in the valid quantum pair, uses a common post-font interval, and checks ordered caller/depth/path prefixes. This is much stronger evidence than audio-call totals or unnormalized endpoint counts.
- It retires harmful or completed-question hooks, rejects the pixel and hot-guard regressions, preserves original instructions, and captures exceptions before register context restoration. Native instruction-replay reproduction was valuable engineering.
- It preserves failed runs and distinguishes bounded stop, host termination, missing native final, and actual emulator exception. Launcher success is not being used as application success in the retained handoffs.
- It preserves original firmware/app/libraries and the installed historical runtime, uses existing tools and copy-on-write writable seed copies, and retains ownership-aware finite shutdown. The targeted evidence reviewed here records closure/reaping and postflight even for failures. This is necessary experimental hygiene.
- The workflow reset already removes repeated whole-history inventories, per-run archives, duplicate ledgers and unnecessary new permission prompts. The problem now is implementing that reset consistently and keeping the active knowledge accurate.

**What it needs to do next, in order.** These are recommendations; this review authorizes and executes no additional guest.

1. **Finish the already authorized active plan.** Do not add a replacement guest or a new comparative experiment merely because this review found something interesting. Preserve its attempt limits and selected observation durations. Use corrected effective supervision. Record the older cooperative constructor success alongside its later watchdog/crash failures when the active status is next updated; preserve the distinction between whole-main and the new phase-limited cooperative candidate.
2. **Spend the next useful long observation following construction into original output.** Under the retained better counted condition, follow actual main continuation after the completed boundaries through remaining initialization, an original drawable/window creation, mapping/drawing, and event/input behavior. A ten-minute run is justified by the observed later boundaries; it is not a requirement to wait ten minutes after a decisive failure. Sparse boundaries and existing captures should answer whether main is doing CPU construction, waiting, or actually rendering. If construction finishes without output, narrow the next question to the observed rendering/initialization boundary. Another general caller census is a poor substitute.
3. **Use the separate phase-limited cooperative comparison to address a demonstrated scheduler reliability problem.** The older whole-main cooperative runs reached the constructor quickly but later failed. The newer candidate changes only early/font scheduling and then reverts, so it deserves its own evaluation. Do not claim its reliability from the older partial runs or from synthetic SVC controls. If a memory fault recurs, retain synchronous PC/register/mapping/stack evidence and investigate that actual failure. Do not assume timer replay, the earlier CSV pointer, or ASCII-looking `0x30303030` share a cause.
4. **Keep profiling subordinate to the next UI boundary.** Retain sparse font, active continuation, main state, worker-service and drawable observations. Disable completed CSV/parent/hot pixel/audio counting where no current question needs them. If throughput remains limiting, make one instrumented comparison of a suspected cost with the same seed and bound. Worker slice increments establish service, not low-latency audio. Correct the clock only if a trace implicates timing. Do not pursue every known fixture imperfection at once.
5. **Make each ordinary iteration cheap.** Use one question, one retained harness revision, explicit duration/count/observer parameters, fresh writable state, and only focused controls for changed contracts. Let the supervisor produce command, identities, raw logs, deadlines and cleanup automatically. Write a short HANDOFF containing the decision, milestone, material failure, evidence and next question; link the automatic records for repeated guard/identity/cleanup detail. The existing two-minute preparation/one-minute reporting targets are useful aspirations, not extra ledgers or gates. A difficult first implementation can exceed them; unchanged repeats should not.
6. **Keep one accurate live status and use reviews selectively.** Update README when the frontier or decision changes. Save requested substantial reviews here and index them, but do not require a full review or four-experiment plan after each routine test. Preserve raw failures with meaningful partial progress so they are discoverable. An already authorized research loop should be able to interpret one result and make its ordinary correction without exporting a new prompt. Use one owner for launch endpoints and shared runtime preparation; any explicitly delegated work should have separate file ownership. Do not introduce simultaneous original guests into performance comparisons.
